The Second Salary

This essay is the basis of the work
The Second Salary

2024

The following research focuses on the ‘Cagi’ factory, which for many years occupied the center of my town, Motta Visconti, a community of approximately 8,000 inhabitants. In the summer of 2023, demolition work on the factory began, and with that my curiosity sparked.

In 1955, in Motta Visconti, the ‘Cagi Maglierie’ was founded: a company that created employment for hundreds of people until 1994. Officially, the headquarters in Motta Visconti opened in a pre-existing building on 12 January 1957, after some renovation works. The factory covered an area of 40,000 cubic meters and included warehouses, offices, commercial area and, as previously mentioned, was located in the town center. The economic impact of the company was considerable, generating well-being in the town, which was much smaller at the time and experienced years of prosperity both in terms of employment and trade. This activity also brought new opportunities and well-being to families, because it provided a second income. In fact, mainly workers and employed women were hired, young girls from 15 years and older. This inevitably represented a step towards the independence of a new working class, as well as welfare for families. The headquarters in Motta Visconti had 450 female employees, most of whom lived in the town and therefore without travel expenses. Other workers came from neighboring towns: Casorate, Bereguardo and Trivolzio. The site closed around 2014. In the years before the closure some people were transferred to Cilavegna, where the fabric factory was located, while in Motta Visconti two workshops remained. There was talk of closure already in 1977, in fact since then, the company had stopped hiring staff. In early August 2012, after more than 50 years of production, the “Cagi” was completely acquired by the French CSP International Fashion Group. After taking photographs of the site where the factory once stood, I wanted to deepen, listening to the testimonies of some of the workers who worked in the plant, to better understand their work and life experiences that has changed for some decades the face of my country. I report later parts of some interviews.

Interview with Celario Innocentina 1953

“....I worked at Cagi from 1977 to 2008/2009 as a worker, after my hiring only two other people were hired. Initially I counted the cut garments that had to be delivered to the laboratory, where they would then be sewn; the work was abundant. There were several departments: sewing, ironing, packaging and shipping; there were also those who cut labels and those who dealt with folding. We produced underwear and pajamas, the best-known garments, and I still remember are the code 1808 briefs, the cod.1313 tank top and a mercerized V-neck half sleeve shirt. After that I worked for a short time in the bending department, and then I was moved to shipping. The department was located in the basement, where men also worked, including 3 mechanics and some warehouse workers. Shipments went to supermarkets such as “Bennet” and “Esselunga” but also abroad....”

Interview with Azzimonti Ernesta 1940

“...I started working at “Cagi” in Motta in 1955, and worked there until 1987, for 32 years. I prepared the garments that were to be sent out of the laboratory, where they were cut and then rearranged. The “Cagi” had several laboratories: in Serravalle, in Palestro, in Mede and in Cilavegna where yarns were produced, in Motta Visconti we made the packaging. We had a wool knitwear department; a department where we produced men’s briefs, men’s tank tops, another for women’s underpants, children’s shirts, wool underwear and machines that made tubular. Also t-shirts for the designer Roberta Di Camerino. They were expensive products, the brand was expensive...”

Interview with Celestina Cavalli 1939

“...the name “Cagi” comes from the Calzificio Giudice that had its seat in Cilavegna, before becoming Cag knitwear. The headquarters in Cilavegna was very large, more than that of Motta. In the headquarters in Motta Visconti the first workers were hired in ‘42, first in Milan, then being transferred to Motta Visconti. I started working there in ‘54, at 15. I was assigned no. 82. They hired not over 18, and the minimum age was 15. Mr Gerosa ran and set up a company with only female employees; we village girls were far from those of the city. 425 hired plus other laboratories around, together they counted 1000 workers. The working hours were from 8.00 to 17.30, but you could work even 9 hours (9 hours a day when they were sponge Argentines); also you worked on Saturday, all day. Initially I took 46 lire per hour. I made 500,000 lire per month. The delays were obviously deducted from my salary. I remember the first strike we made, there were also female workers who came from Besate. Depending on the task we had a uniform of different colors. In white those who worked on the production machines, while the ‘unproductive’ had the black apron. I remember when they tried to introduce the use of computers: the first time was a failure, while the second time nobody wanted to learn, but I did. I then went to Cilavegna to teach how to use the computer between ‘85 and ‘86. They tried to open another department in Motta Visconti towards the coast of Ticino but the City did not give him permission; then they opened in Rosate...”

Interview with Brusati Giuseppina 1943

“I started working at the Cagi in Motta Visconti in ‘58, at 15, and soon after they closed the hiring. At first they put me in the cutting department where rolls of fabric arrived. There was a machine that stretched fabric and the workers with the paper pattern cut the garments. Then always in the cutting department they put me to make tags, e.g. 1200 briefs man, packs of the model and then they went to the sewing machines. Then they moved me to the expeditions, which left for central or southern Italy; but also for the department stores such as the “Standa” that required special procedures for shipments. On the ground floor there were sewing machines and the shipping department, but below was the fabric warehouse. We also had an ironing and folding department. In the Piana department (named after the director of the department) there were machines and fabrics for tubular women’s shirts. Finally, I was assigned to work alongside Mr. Giulio Gerosa, the director. I worked at Cagi for over 30 years, until I was 56 years old. I retired but continued to work as a consultant for two years...”
